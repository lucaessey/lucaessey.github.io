Captain Barnaby & the Bloytoff

1. A captain of absolutely nothing

Panel 1

A very proud red chicken stands on a bucket in a muddy pen while ordinary hens look on.

Barnaby was a red chicken with about three working brain cells. All three were convinced he was a world-famous pirate captain.

Panel 2

Barnaby puts a folded paper coupon hat on his comb and sets off toward the forest.

The other chickens said he lived in a muddy pen behind a Denny’s. Barnaby didn’t care. He made a pirate hat from an old Arby’s coupon and marched into the woods to find treasure.

Panel 3

Barnaby staggers backward as a tiny dandelion showers him with seeds beside a pinecone.

Ten feet into the woods, he was already lost. He pecked a dry pinecone and lost a fight with a dandelion.

Panel 4

Barnaby stretches his neck and opens his beak; startled birds scatter from the pine trees.

Then he crowed at the top of his lungs. It sounded like an angry kazoo being dropped down an escalator.

Sound: KRAA-KAZOO!

2. A terrible week for Rufus

Panel 5

An exhausted spotted bobcat examines a white golf ball and holds up his sore paw.

Rufus the bobcat was having the worst week of his life. He’d spent three hours stalking a toad that turned out to be a golf ball. He also had a splinter in his paw.

Panel 6

Rufus licks his lips high on a pine branch, watching the little chicken pass beneath him.

Now a loud, red chicken dinner had walked right under his tree.

Rufus: I’m going to eat your face off.

Panel 7

Barnaby gestures grandly with a tiny wing at a deeply confused Rufus in the tree.

Barnaby tilted his head and focused one dumb, beady eye on the terrifying predator.

Barnaby: Prepare to be boarded, you hairy potato.

Panel 8

Rufus pauses mid-pounce on his branch as a bush below rustles mysteriously.

Usually things screamed or ran. Rufus crouched, wiggling his butt to pounce. Then the ground started making wet, sloppy fart noises.

Sound: SPLAT. SQUEAK. GLUG. TOOT.

3. Enter the aggressive mistake

Panel 9

A purple fuzzy creature emerges from the leaves on four webbed feet, with crossed eyes and a long orange trumpet snout.

Out wobbled a Bloytoff: an aggressive mistake. A lumpy, twenty-pound purple beanbag. Four stubby webbed feet. Zero neck. Crossed eyes that spun like roulette wheels. Its party-horn snout wheezed warm steam.

Panel 10

Rufus tumbles through a tangle of branches toward a bush, all four paws flailing.

Rufus was so horrified that his back leg slipped. He fell twelve feet, hit four limbs on the way down, and landed in prickly briars.

Sound: OOF.

Panel 11

Barnaby proudly presents the purple creature, which is far more interested in a leaf.

The Bloytoff ignored him and tried to eat a dead leaf.

Barnaby: Ah! My trusty steed has arrived!

Panel 12

A leaf sticks inside the Bloytoff’s horn as its face scrunches into an enormous sneeze.

The leaf went straight up its trumpet nose. Its face scrunched up…

Sound: KA-CHOO!

4. An unfortunate collision

Panel 13

The Bloytoff skids backward along a dirt furrow while its snout blasts a white sneeze cloud the other way.

The recoil rocketed it backward four feet, scraping its butt across the dirt like a purple hovercraft operating in reverse.

Panel 14

Twigs caught in his fur, Rufus charges at the little red chicken.

Rufus dragged himself out of the briars: sticks on his forehead, pine needles in his nose, dignity completely gone. He roared and charged.

Sound: RRAAARGH!

Panel 15

Barnaby flutters straight upward while Rufus slides beneath him on loose pine needles.

Barnaby didn’t run. He flapped his scrawny wings and did an awkward chicken-hop straight up into the air.

Panel 16

The bobcat’s face sinks into the creature’s soft purple belly, making its trumpet nose spring up.

Rufus missed, lost his footing, and plowed face-first into the Bloytoff’s squishy, jiggling belly.

Sound: HONK!

5. Mint condition

Panel 17

The Bloytoff balloons into a round purple puff, narrowing its eyes as it inhales through its horn.

The Bloytoff did not like being a pillow. Its eyes snapped into focus. Its body puffed up like a microwave popcorn bag. It took one massive, whistling breath…

Panel 18

Three bright blue blobs fly from the Bloytoff’s orange snout toward the startled bobcat.

…and fired its ultimate defense: three massive, lukewarm globs of bright blue, mint-flavored toothpaste-jelly. Both eyeballs. And down his throat.

Sound: THWIP-THWIP-THWIP!

Panel 19

Rufus flails his paws with blue mint jelly over his eyes and toothpaste foam bubbling from his mouth.

He smacked his own face and sneezed foam…

Rufus: IT TASTES LIKE MOUTHWASTE!

Panel 20

Rufus bumps into a pine tree with blue goo still on his face and little cartoon stars circling his head.

…and ran straight into a tree trunk.

Sound: BONK.

6. Full speed… backward

Panel 21

Barnaby leaps above the Bloytoff, his skinny chicken feet outstretched for a dramatic landing.

Seeing pure chaos, Barnaby decided this was his cue to conquer. He jumped, gave a wild pirate scream…

Panel 22

Barnaby stands on the Bloytoff’s back, gripping its fur with his toes; the creature’s eyes widen in alarm.

…and slammed his chicken feet onto the Bloytoff’s back. Sticky mint goo everywhere. A screaming cat. A chicken riding its spine. The Bloytoff panicked.

Panel 23

Rapid sneeze clouds shoot out to the right while the purple creature and its chicken rider rocket backward to the left.

Emergency turbo mode. Every sneeze launched them backward at thirty miles an hour.

Sound: ACHOO! ACHOO! ACHOO!

Panel 24

Barnaby rides joyfully through the forest as his paper hat flies off behind him.

Barnaby was having the time of his life on this backward-firing lint roller. His toes held on. His Arby’s hat flew off.

Barnaby: FULL SPEED AHEAD, YOU GLORIOUS HAM!

7. The maiden voyage

Panel 25

The now hatless chicken and purple creature burst through bushes across a forest path, scattering leaves.

Backward through bushes. Backward across a dirt trail.

Panel 26

They shoot backward off a small muddy ledge above a blue creek.

And right off the edge of a five-foot muddy cliff.

Panel 27

Barnaby and the Bloytoff hang in sunlit air above the still water, all their feet dangling.

For two beautiful seconds, they were airborne.

Panel 28

An enormous plume of muddy blue water erupts where the pair lands.

Then they hit the creek. Mud and water splashed thirty feet in the air.

Sound: KER-BLAM!

8. A change of diet

Panel 29

The soggy red chicken stands triumphantly on the floating purple creature, which blows bubbles in the creek.

When the water settled, the Bloytoff floated like a giant purple pool donut, happily blowing bubbles through its trumpet nose. Barnaby stood on its head: dripping, duckweed-covered, and puffing his chest out in pure victory.

Panel 30

A battered-looking but uninjured Rufus reaches the creek with blue goo over one eye and an exasperated stare.

Rufus stumbled down to the bank. One eye was still blinded by mint slime. His forehead sported a massive bump. He stared at the chicken standing on a purring, farting purple water-balloon.

Panel 31

Covered in duckweed, Barnaby spits a little algae and squawks proudly at Rufus from the Bloytoff’s head.

Barnaby locked eyes with the bobcat, spat out a piece of algae, and gave one last pitiful, kazoo-sounding squawk.

Sound: …kraa-kazoo.

Panel 32

Rufus weeps on the bank and brings a blade of grass to his mouth while Barnaby and the Bloytoff float behind him.

Rufus sat in the mud, put his paws over his face, and quietly cried. Then he dragged himself away to eat some grass.

Rufus: I’m going to become a vegetarian.

Read the original story

Barnaby was a red chicken who had about three working brain cells, and all three of them were convinced he was a world-famous pirate captain.

The other chickens tried to tell him that he lived in a muddy pen behind a Denny’s, but Barnaby didn't care. He made a paper pirate hat out of an old Arby's coupon, shoved it onto his comb, and marched straight into the woods to find treasure.

Ten feet into the woods, Barnaby was already lost. He pecked a dry pinecone, lost a fight with a dandelion, and crowed at the top of his lungs. His crow didn't sound like a rooster; it sounded like an angry kazoo being dropped down an escalator.

Up in a pine tree, a bobcat named Rufus was having the worst week of his life. He had spent three hours stalking a toad only to find out it was a golf ball, he had a splinter in his paw, and now a loud, red chicken dinner had just walked right under his tree.

"I’m going to eat your face off," Rufus whispered, licking his chops.

Barnaby tilted his head sideways, focused one dumb, beady eye on the terrifying predator, and said, "Prepare to be boarded, you hairy potato."

Rufus paused. Usually things screamed or ran. He crouched low, wiggling his butt to get ready to pounce, when the ground suddenly started making wet, sloppy fart noises.

SPLAT. SQUEAK. GLUG. TOOT.

A bush parted, and out wobbled a Bloytoff.

A Bloytoff looked like an aggressive mistake. It was basically a lumpy, twenty-pound beanbag made of bright purple fuzz, with four stubby webbed feet, zero neck, and two crossed eyes that spun around like roulette wheels. In the middle of its face was a long snout that looked exactly like a plastic party horn, which continuously wheezed and leaked warm steam.

Rufus was so utterly horrified by this creature that his back leg slipped off the branch. He fell twelve feet through the air, hit four limbs on the way down, and landed directly in a pile of prickly briars with an embarrassing OOF.

Barnaby looked at the purple fuzzball. "Ah! My trusty steed has arrived!"

The Bloytoff ignored Barnaby and tried to eat a dead leaf. The leaf went up its trumpet nose. The Bloytoff's face scrunched up, and it let out a violent, deafening sneeze. KA-CHOO!

The recoil was so strong that the Bloytoff rocketed backward four feet, scraping its butt across the dirt like a purple hovercraft operating in reverse.

Rufus dragged himself out of the briars. He had sticks stuck to his forehead, pine needles in his nose, and his dignity was completely gone. He let out an angry roar and charged Barnaby at full speed.

Barnaby didn't even try to run. He just flapped his scrawny wings in a panic and did an awkward chicken-hop straight up into the air.

Rufus missed the chicken entirely, lost his footing on the pine needles, and plowed face-first into the Bloytoff’s squishy, jiggling belly.

HONK! went the Bloytoff.

The Bloytoff did not like being used as a pillow. Its crossed eyes snapped into the center of its face with terrifying focus. Its entire body puffed up like a microwave popcorn bag about to pop. It took a massive, whistling breath through its party-horn snout and fired its ultimate defense weapon.

THWIP-THWIP-THWIP!

Three massive, lukewarm globs of bright blue, mint-flavored toothpaste-jelly hit Rufus squarely in both eyeballs and down his throat.

Rufus gagged. "IT TASTES LIKE MOUTHWASTE!" he shrieked, blindly smacking his own face, sneezing foam, and running straight into a tree trunk. BONK.

Seeing pure chaos, Barnaby decided this was his cue to conquer. He jumped into the air, gave a wild pirate scream, and slammed his chicken feet right onto the Bloytoff’s back.

The Bloytoff panicked. It had sticky mint goo everywhere, a screaming cat next to it, and a chicken riding its spine. It went into emergency turbo mode and started machine-gun sneezing:

ACHOO! ACHOO! ACHOO! ACHOO!

Every sneeze launched them backward at thirty miles an hour.

Barnaby was having the time of his life. He was riding a purple, backward-firing lint roller through the forest at dangerous speeds, holding on with his toes, his Arby's pirate hat flying off, screaming, "FULL SPEED AHEAD, YOU GLORIOUS HAM!"

They shot backward through bushes, backwards across a dirt trail, and right off the edge of a five-foot muddy cliff.

For two beautiful seconds, they were airborne. Then they hit the creek.

KER-BLAM!

Mud and water splashed thirty feet in the air.

When the water settled, the Bloytoff was floating like a giant purple pool donut, happily blowing bubbles through its trumpet nose. Barnaby was standing on top of its head, dripping wet, covered in duckweed, looking like a drowned rat, but puffing his chest out in pure victory.

Rufus, totally blind in one eye from mint slime and sporting a massive bump on his forehead from the tree, finally stumbled down to the creek bank.

He stared at the soaking wet chicken standing on a purring, farting purple water-balloon.

Barnaby locked eyes with the bobcat, spat out a piece of algae, and let out one more pitiful, kazoo-sounding squawk.

Rufus sat down in the mud, put his paws over his face, and began to quietly cry. "I'm going to become a vegetarian," he whimpered, and dragged himself away to eat some grass.
